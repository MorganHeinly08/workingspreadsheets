How it's calculated

The calculator counts overnights: where the children sleep each night of the year.

pattern nights = (parent's nights in the 14-night cycle ÷ 14) × nights outside summer

overnights = pattern nights + summer nights given to that parent ± holiday nights moved

share = parent's overnights ÷ (Parent A overnights + Parent B overnights)

Every night belongs to one parent, so the totals always add up to the year and the share equals overnights ÷ 365 (or 366).

Adjustments replace nights; they never add to them. In a summer week given to Parent B, the regular pattern would already have given Parent B some nights. So the calculator takes those 7 nights out of the pattern first, then gives all 7 to Parent B. Adding 7 on top would count some nights twice. A holiday night moved from Parent A to Parent B comes off one total and onto the other.

The figures are averages, which is why they carry a decimal. A year is 26 full cycles plus one night (two in a leap year), so a date-by-date count on a real calendar can differ by up to one night (two in a leap year), depending on the day the cycle starts.

Worked example

Every other weekend, Friday to Monday morning: Parent B has the Friday, Saturday and Sunday nights of every second week, 3 of every 14 nights.

3 ÷ 14 × 365 = 78.2 overnights for Parent B and 286.8 for Parent A. As shares, 78.2 ÷ 365 = 21.4% and 78.6%.

Now give Parent B two summer weeks. Those 14 nights come out of the pattern, leaving 351. Parent B's pattern share is 3 ÷ 14 × 351 = 75.2, plus the 14 summer nights: 89.2 overnights (24.4%). Adding 14 to 78.2 would give 92.2, counting the 3 pattern nights in those weeks twice.

Move two holiday nights from Parent A to Parent B as well, and Parent B has 91.2 (25.0%) while Parent A falls by the same two nights, to 273.8.

Common questions

How do I calculate custody percentage?

Count each parent's overnights in a year and divide by the nights in the year. For a schedule that repeats every two weeks there is a shortcut: a parent's nights in the 14-night cycle divided by 14. Three nights in 14 is 21.4%; seven in 14 is 50%.

What percentage is every other weekend?

It depends on when the weekend ends. Friday to Sunday evening is 2 overnights every 14 nights: 52.1 a year, 14.3%. Friday to Monday morning is 3: 78.2 a year, 21.4%. Adding one midweek overnight every week to the 2-night version gives 104.3 a year, 28.6%.

What percentage is a 2-2-3 schedule?

50% for each parent: 7 of every 14 nights, or 182.5 overnights in a 365-night year. The 2-2-5-5, 3-4-4-3 and alternating-weeks schedules come out at 50% as well; they differ in how often the children move between homes, not in the total.

Does a daytime visit or a dinner count?

Not in this calculator, which counts overnights only. Some places give credit for daytime care or count hours or days instead, so a parent with substantial daytime time may have a different figure under their local rules.

Is this the number a court or support worksheet will use?

Not necessarily. Jurisdictions measure parenting time in different ways and set their own thresholds, and this calculator does not calculate child support. Use it to understand a schedule, then check the figure against the rules that apply to you with your attorney or mediator.

Is anything I type stored or sent anywhere?

No. It runs entirely in your browser and nothing is sent to a server. If you copy the page link after changing the inputs, the inputs travel in the link itself.

Honest scope

This is a planning aid, not legal advice, and it does not calculate child support.

States and countries measure parenting time differently. Many count overnights; some count days or partial days, some count hours, and some give credit for daytime care. Where parenting time affects child support, the thresholds that matter, and what happens when one is crossed, vary as well. Check the guideline or worksheet that applies to you, ideally with your attorney or mediator, before relying on any figure.

The figures assume the schedule runs exactly as written all year. Real years include swapped weekends, travel and illness, so a projection and a record of what happened are different things, and both can be worth having.
